Though I am interested in a wide range of research areas, I am most excited by research efforts that use existing tools in clever or creative ways to accomplish some new science, especially in the domain of high energy astrophysics, and even more specifically on Neutron Stars. My experience so far has taught me: how to review literature proficiently; how to create software (primarily in C++ and Python) following open-science practices; how to present my work at colloqia/conferences, and how to take feedback from those presentations; how to write, submit, get rejected, revise, and resubmit publications as first author.

### Neutron Star Interiors

* **Pre-print**: O'Connor, Huang \& Chen. _Combining the Mass--Radius Posteriors of PSR J0030+0451 Allowing for Unknown Model Systematics_ https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2604.1, April 2026

* Under the mentorship of Prof. Alexander Chen and Chun Huang, I developed a Bayesian framework for coherently combining Mass-Radius posteriors of an individual neutron star, using data from the Neutron Star Interior Composition ExploreR (NICER). For this work I was granted the [M.R. Metzger Award for Undergraduate Research](https://physics.washu.edu/news/undergraduate-research-opening-doors-sparking-new-ideas-and-shaping-next-generation-physicists).


### FRB Counterparts with VERITAS

* For my senior thesis project, I am developing a simulation pipeline for the Very Energetic Radiation Imaging Telescope Array (VERITAS) Optical Monitoring Upgrade, to demonstrate the detection of MeV-GeV, millisecond-duration "wavefront" emission that may accompany Fast Radio Bursts. The accompanying paper is currently in the writing phase.

* **GitHub**: `VERITAS-Wavefront`, O'Connor 2026. A fork of the [`VTS-SimPipe`](https://github.com/VERITAS-Observatory/VTS-SimPipe) repository, models the arrival of many simultaneous gamma-rays and their detection using per-pixel electronic variance. https://github.com/ryan-oconnor-astro/VERITAS-Wavefront
